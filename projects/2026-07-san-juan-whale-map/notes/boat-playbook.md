# Boat-day playbook (heuristics)

Not a guarantee. Distilled for a rental-boat group around San Juan Island. Cross-check live reports the morning you leave.

## Species & season (Central Salish Sea / San Juan)

| Who | Diet / behavior | When odds are usually better near SJI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Southern Resident orcas (J/K/L) | Chinook-focused; often travel west-side Haro | Historically summer–early fall in central Salish Sea; presence has shifted year-to-year — check recent reports |
| Bigg’s (transient) orcas | Hunt seals, sea lions, porpoise | Year-round; often near haul-outs (e.g. Cattle Pass / southern tips) |
| Humpback | Lunge / bubble feeding when bait balls | Increasing since ~2010; spring–fall; Juan de Fuca approaches strong |
| Minke | Solo, elusive | Sporadic spring–fall |
| Gray | Mostly coast / migration; fewer inland | Shoulder months; not the main SJI bet |

Peak tourist whale-watch weather is roughly May–September; that is also when small-boat sea state is most often workable.

## Classic water to put the boat in

1. **Haro Strait, west side of San Juan** — Lime Kiln → San Juan County Park → Mitchell Bay approaches. Deep water close to shore; classic Southern Resident travel corridor.
2. **Southern island / Cattle Pass** — seal & sea lion habitat → Bigg’s hunting grounds (NPS land guide notes this).
3. **Spieden Channel / northern passes** — secondary; watch currents.
4. **Eastern straits (San Juan Channel)** — more boat traffic; whales pass through but west-side Haro is the higher-yield default for Residents.

## Time of day

No magic hour with strong evidence in the opportunistic datasets. Practical constraints dominate:

- Morning often calmer wind → better for small boats and binoculars.
- Plan a 4–6 hour window so you can relocate if the morning corridor is empty.
- Folk pattern to test (not proven here): west-side travel tied to tidal current direction — check Friday Harbor tide and note what you observe.

## Weather & sea state

- Haro kicks up fast with westerlies / northwesters. If small-craft or wind advisory, favor leeward shores or postpone — whales will still be there tomorrow.
- Fog: whales don’t care; you lose visual range and ferry/shipping risk rises.
- Bring layers; wind chill on the water is not the Friday Harbor dock forecast.

## Rules that raise everyone’s odds long-term

- **Killer whales:** stay ≥ 200 yards (183 m) in Washington inland waters; slower when in the vicinity ([Be Whale Wise](https://www.bewhalewise.org/)).
- Do not park on the animals’ path; approach from the side/rear parallel, then go neutral if they turn toward you.
- Keep a listening watch on VHF; commercial boats often know — but don’t create a pile-up.

## Launch / logistics pointers

| Launch / harbor | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Friday Harbor | Most rentals; longer run to west-side Haro |
| Roche Harbor / Snug Harbor | Shorter hop to north/west corridors |
| Anacortes | Mainland start; budget crossing time + fuel |

Fuel plan for a west-side loop with reserve. Cell coverage is patchy mid-Haro — download the map offline before casting off.

## Morning checklist

1. Acartia / Orca Network / tour-operator social for last 24 h.
2. OrcaSound Lime Kiln — any overnight calls?
3. NWS marine forecast + Friday Harbor tide.
4. Pick primary corridor (usually west-side) + Bigg’s backup (Cattle Pass seals) if Residents are reported elsewhere.
5. Binoculars, camera with zoom, dry bags, float plan left with someone ashore.
